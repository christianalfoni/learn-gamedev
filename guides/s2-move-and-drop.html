Move and drop through

Run, jump, and drop through the floors. The mechanics of getting around the arena, built plainly — because in this game reaching an enemy's floor is the skill, and you can't tune that until you can do it.

Godot 4.3+ 45–75 min 6 steps base game

What the game will have that it doesn't now

  • A player that runs and jumps around a multi-floor arena.
  • Drop-through: press down and fall through the floor you're standing on.
  • A facing value the shooting assignment will read, since you fire the way you face.
  • A measured traversal time from the top floor to the bottom and back — the number the whole game balances against.

What this deliberately leaves plain

No acceleration, no friction, no coyote time, no input buffering, no jump derived from height and time — just constants that work. Movement feel is the single most important thing in this game and it gets a whole assignment to itself later, where it can be tuned against a real arena with real pressure. Making it feel good now would mean tuning it twice.

What you need

An arena with walls on collision layer 1 and one-way floors on collision layer 2. If you don't have one, two StaticBody2D platforms and a floor will do for this assignment — set the platform's CollisionShape2D → One Way Collision on, and put it on collision layer 2 via the node's Collision → Layer in the inspector.

Step 1

The input map

6 min

Project → Project Settings → Input Map. Four actions:

  • move_left — A, ←
  • move_right — D, →
  • jump — Space, W, ↑
  • drop — S, ↓

Add fire too while you're here — J or the left mouse button — even though nothing reads it yet. It costs nothing now and saves you coming back.

Separate jump and drop, on purpose

Plenty of platformers use down+jump together to drop through. In this game you'll be dropping constantly — it's the fast route down and the fast route down is most of your routing — so it deserves its own key rather than a two-key chord you have to be careful with under pressure.

Step 2

The player scene

10 min
  1. Scene → New Scene → Other Node → CharacterBody2D, rename it Player.
  2. Add a CollisionShape2D with a RectangleShape2D, roughly 10 × 14 — narrower than a tile, so you fit through gaps without catching on the edges.
  3. Add a ColorRect sized to match, or any sprite. A coloured box is the right level of art for this assignment.
  4. Save as player.tscn and instance it into the arena near the bottom.

Keep the collision box narrow

If the player's box is as wide as a tile, dropping through a one-tile gap becomes a pixel-perfect alignment puzzle. Two pixels of clearance on each side turns "aim carefully" into "go down", which is what you want when the reason you're moving is that three enemies are about to reach the drain.

Step 3

Run and jump

12 min

Attach a script to Player, save it as player.gd, and replace everything with this:

Run it, and check one thing only

Can you jump from one floor to the one above it? If not, raise JUMP_VELOCITY (more negative) until you can, comfortably, without having to be precise. Everything else about how this feels — the instant stops, the floaty arc — is a known problem with a known owner, and it isn't this assignment.

Y points down

In Godot 2D, +Y is downward, so the jump velocity is negative and gravity is positive. If your character rockets into the floor on jump, that's the sign.

Step 4

Drop through the floor

15 min

One-way floors are solid from above and open from below. To go down through one, the player has to stop colliding with floors for a moment — and only with floors, never with walls. That's why they're on their own collision layer.

Call it from _physics_process, just before move_and_slide().

If the timer is too short, you snap back up

Re-enabling the mask while the player is still overlapping a floor pushes them back out of it, which reads as the drop mysteriously failing. If that happens, raise DROP_TIME a little rather than fighting it. Too long is also a bug, though a quieter one: you'll fall through two floors at once and wonder why your routing feels imprecise.

The thing to notice

Drop, then drop again, then jump back up. That loop — down fast, up slow — is the shape of every decision you'll make in this game. Going down is cheap and going up is expensive, so being caught on the wrong floor is a real cost. That asymmetry is the game working.

Why facing persists when you stop

In a game where you shoot the way you walk, standing still has to mean "still aimed that way". If facing reset to a default on release, you'd stop to line up a shot and the gun would swing away from the thing you stopped for.

Step 6

Time the arena

12 min

This is the assignment. Not "does it move" — how long does it take to get anywhere, because that number is what the whole game is balanced against.

Run it and time yourself, out loud or with a phone, on each of these:

RouteWhy it matters
Top floor to the bottom, dropping all the wayYour best-case interception time. Nothing can escape faster than this or the game is unwinnable.
Bottom to the top, jumping all the wayThe expensive direction. If this is more than about three times the drop, being caught low is punishing.
One floor down and back upThe move you'll make most. It should feel routine, not like a commitment.
End to end along one floorHow long a walker gets to live if you're on its floor already.

Now compare the first number with the descent time you measured when you built the arena. If a falling object reaches the drain faster than you can cross the arena, the game is asking for something impossible — and the fix is almost always the arena, not the player. Move a gap so the route down is less direct, rather than making the player faster.

The one that tells you the most

Bottom to top. It's the movement you'll resent, and how much you resent it decides whether the two health bars create tension or frustration. Too slow and defending the base means abandoning yourself; too fast and there's no cost to being in the wrong place, which means no decision to make.

Write the four numbers down. When the movement-feel assignment comes, these are the before-and-after, and they're far more useful than remembering it "felt sluggish".

That's the assignment

The game now has someone in it, and you know what the arena costs to cross. That second part is what makes every later balance conversation concrete rather than a matter of impressions.
