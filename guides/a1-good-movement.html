Sketch A1 · Module A — Godot game-feel

The good-movement character

A character that feels great to move, and nothing else. No goal, no enemies, no score. By the end you'll have eight tuning knobs in the inspector and strong opinions about where each one should sit.

Godot 4.5+ 45–90 min 8 steps GDScript

What you'll have at the end

  • A CharacterBody2D that accelerates, coasts and stops on its own curve rather than snapping between 0 and full speed.
  • A jump defined in the units you actually think in — "64 pixels high, 0.4 seconds up, 0.3 seconds down" — instead of a magic gravity number.
  • Coyote time and jump buffering: the two cheapest tricks in platforming, and the reason good games feel fair.
  • A short tuning session where you deliberately make it feel bad, so you can tell what each knob is doing.

11 minutes of watching before you open Godot. Both are worth it.

Step 1

The input map

10 min

New project, 2D. Before any code: Project → Project Settings → Input Map. Add three actions and bind them:

  • move_left — A and ←
  • move_right — D and →
  • jump — Space

An action can hold as many bindings as you like, which is the whole point — add a gamepad binding too if you have one plugged in. Click + next to the action, then press the key or stick you want.

Why this comes first

Reading Input.is_key_pressed(KEY_SPACE) works and will poison every later sketch. Actions are the layer where rebinding, gamepads and accessibility live. Ten minutes now, or a refactor in every project you ever start.

Step 2

The player scene, and something to fall off

10 min

Two scenes. First the player, on its own, because a scene is Godot's prefab:

  1. Scene → New Scene → Other Node → CharacterBody2D. Rename the root to Player.
  2. Add a CollisionShape2D child. In the inspector, Shape → New RectangleShape2D, size roughly 16 × 24.
  3. Add a Sprite2D child and drag icon.svg from the FileSystem dock onto its Texture. Scale it down to about 0.2 so it roughly matches the box. Placeholder art is not cheating — this sketch is about movement.
  4. Save as player.tscn.

Then a world to move around in: New Scene → 2D Scene, save as world.tscn, and set it as the main scene when Godot offers. Instance the player into it with the chain-link Instantiate Child Scene button.

For the floor, add a StaticBody2D with a CollisionShape2D using a wide, flat RectangleShape2D. Then — and this matters for step 6 — duplicate it into a raised ledge with a clear edge to walk off. Without an edge you cannot feel coyote time.

Easy to miss

A CollisionShape2D with no shape assigned silently does nothing and Godot only warns you with a small yellow triangle in the scene tree. If your player falls through the world, check both shapes before you check your code.

Step 3

Movement with no feel at all

5 min

Attach a script to the Player root, save it as player.gd, and replace everything Godot generated with this:

Input.get_axis(negative, positive) returns a single float from -1 to 1, which saves you writing two branches. velocity is a property that CharacterBody2D already owns; move_and_slide() reads it, moves the body, and resolves collisions. The parameter is _delta with an underscore because we don't use it yet — that's how you tell Godot to stop warning about it.

Run it — F5 — and feel this

Full speed on the exact frame you press, zero on the exact frame you release. It handles like dragging a window, not like moving a body. Hold that feeling in mind; everything from here is a reaction to it.

Step 4

Acceleration and friction

12 min

Instead of assigning speed, move toward it at a limited rate:

The units are the useful part

Acceleration is in pixels per second squared, so max_speed / acceleration is literally the seconds it takes to reach top speed: 300 / 2000 = 0.15 s. Friction 2400 stops you from full speed in 0.125 s. Stopping slightly faster than starting is a good default — it reads as controlled rather than slippery.

Run it and feel this

Now try acceleration = 600 (ice) and acceleration = 8000 (near-instant, but softer than step 3). Change friction to 400 and try to stop on a specific spot. This is the sketch working — you're not implementing, you're listening.

Step 5

A jump you can actually tune

15 min

Most tutorials hand you gravity = 980 and JUMP_VELOCITY = -400, then leave you guessing what happens if you want a jump that's a bit higher. Those aren't the units you think in. You think: "about four tiles high, and it should hang a little at the top." So export those and derive the rest.

For constant gravity g and an upward launch speed v, the apex arrives at t = v / g and the height reached is h = v² / 2g. Solve for what you want to type in:

The two lines of algebra

v = 2h / t and g = 2h / t² — where h is the height you want and t is how long you want the rise to take. Use a different t on the way down and you get asymmetric gravity: floaty up, decisive down. That single trick is most of what separates a good jump from a realistic one.

Y points down

In Godot's 2D space, +Y is downward. So velocity.y < 0.0 means rising, and the jump velocity is negative. Recomputing the gravity from the exports every frame costs nothing and buys you something valuable in step 8: you can drag jump_height while the game is running and watch the arc change.

Run it and feel this

Set jump_time_to_fall equal to jump_time_to_peak and jump a few times. Then set it to 0.2. The second one feels like a game; the first feels like a physics demo. Nothing else changed.

Step 6

Coyote time

8 min

Walk off your ledge and press jump a hair late. Nothing happens, and it feels like the game's fault — because it is. Keep a small timer that says "you were on the floor recently enough."

Remember to pass delta at the call site now: _jump(delta).

Zero the timer when you jump

If you don't set _coyote = 0.0 inside the jump, the timer is still positive on the next frame and you get a free double-jump. Test it by mashing.

Step 7

Jump buffering, and a jump you can cut short

8 min

Coyote time forgives pressing slightly late. Buffering forgives pressing slightly early — you hit jump just before landing, and the game remembers it for a moment instead of dropping it. Same shape, opposite direction.

Note what happened to the jump condition: it no longer asks "was the button pressed this frame?" It asks "is there a remembered press, and were we recently on the ground?" Two independent timers, one and. That's the whole trick.

The last two lines are variable jump height: release early while still rising and the upward velocity is halved, so a tap gives a small hop and a hold gives the full arc. Three lines, and it's the difference between one jump and a range of them.

Run it and feel this

Deliberately press jump a fraction before you land, repeatedly, and try to keep a rhythm going up a set of steps. Then set both coyote_time and buffer_time to 0 and do it again. The second version is the one most first-time platformers ship, and it feels broken once you know why.

Step 8

The tuning session

15 min

This is the actual sketch. Everything above was setup so this part could exist.

Tune it while it runs

Start the game, then in the editor switch the Scene dock from Local to Remote and select the running Player. The inspector now edits the live game. Drag jump_height while jumping. This is the single most useful Godot workflow habit in this whole sketch — remote values reset when you stop, so write down the ones you like.

KnobStartPush it hereWhat you're listening for
max_speed300150 / 600Top speed alone changes surprisingly little. Prove it to yourself.
acceleration2000600 / 8000Weight. Low is ice, high is a cursor.
friction2400400 / 9999Precision. Can you stop on a chosen tile?
jump_height6432 / 128Scale. Everything else needs re-tuning after you change this.
jump_time_to_peak0.400.25 / 0.60Hang time. High values feel dreamy and slow to control.
jump_time_to_fall0.300.20 / 0.40Decisiveness of the landing.
jump_cut0.500.20 / 0.90How much range a tap versus a hold gives you.
coyote_time0.120.00 / 0.25Fairness at edges. Above ~0.2 s it starts to feel like cheating.
buffer_time0.120.00 / 0.30Whether fast repeated jumps stay in rhythm.

Work through it in one pass: for each knob, push it to both extremes, then settle somewhere you like. Don't reset between knobs — let the character drift into something that's yours.

Write down the numbers you land on. One line in the sketchbook's notes field is enough. When you re-do A1 in a few weeks, the interesting part is comparing what past-you liked.

That's the sketch

You now have a character with no purpose that is genuinely nice to move around, and — more useful — you can feel the difference between 0.12 and 0.20 seconds of coyote time. That's the skill. The character is disposable.
